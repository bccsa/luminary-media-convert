# Stock-player check — raw verdicts, 2026-08-13 (shared-chunk-chain layout)

Re-run of the five cases against output in the **shared chunk-chain layout** shipped by
Todo section 11: segments packed into `media/<chain>_<n>.m4s` objects at the prefix root,
referenced from per-stream playlists as `../media/…` with `#EXT-X-BYTERANGE`, always fMP4.
The question this run answers: does a stock client notice the layout change? **It does not**
— every verdict matches the 2026-08-11 run against the old per-stream layout.

Engine: hls.js (default config) inside Electron 33 (Chromium), driven headlessly.
Safari-native rows are not part of this run; the 2026-08-11 Safari findings stand for
playlist/key handling, and a native re-check on this layout is cheap to do by hand
(`python3 serve.py 8899`, open in Safari).

### Case 2 under ffmpeg, investigated

`ffmpeg -i master.m3u8` on the multi-angle master reports `Invalid NAL unit size` /
`missing picture in access unit`. Isolation results:

- Each variant playlist decodes **clean in isolation** (both angles, and audio).
- A master with **one** video variant + the audio group: **clean**.
- A master with the **two** video variants and no audio: **corrupt** — and those two
  readers share **no URLs at all** (disjoint chunk chains `v0_*`/`v1_*`, disjoint inits).
- Mirrored locally and demuxed over `file://`: **identical corruption** — not HTTP.

Bytes proven correct three independent ways (isolated decode, range-reconstructed
decode, hls.js playback), and the corruption requires exactly "ffmpeg demuxes two
same-codec video variants concurrently", which no real player does — a player picks
one variant; ffmpeg's CLI demuxer drains every program at once. Conclusion: an
ffmpeg HLS demuxer limitation with concurrent fMP4 byte-range variants, not an
output defect. It is also not new: the 2026-08-11 run observed the same
`Invalid NAL unit size` artefact on the old per-stream layout — "through the
master, never through the media playlist directly" — and dismissed it then
(Todo section 0a). Layout-independent, ffmpeg-internal, and moot for delivery,
where hls.js and Safari are the clients that matter.
